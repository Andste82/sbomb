# What an attribution export must satisfy

This document is the requirement basis for the FOSS milestones. It was written
from the licence texts, the Apache Software Foundation's assembly rules, the
OpenChain specification, the SPDX and CycloneDX schemas, and the SFLC
compliance guides. Sources are at the end.

It states obligations. It does not state legal conclusions, and neither does
sbomb: the manufacturer performs the assessment, the tool supplies the data.

---

## R1 — The document describes exactly what is distributed

> The LICENSE and NOTICE files must exactly represent the contents of the
> distribution they reside in. […] only components and resources actually
> included in a distribution have any bearing on that distribution's NOTICE
> and LICENSE content. — *ASF, Assembling LICENSE and NOTICE files*

Both directions are defects:

* **Under-inclusion** — a shipped component with no notice is a licence breach.
* **Over-inclusion** — naming a GPL component the product does not contain
  invites an obligation that was never triggered, and a source request the
  manufacturer cannot answer.

This is the requirement sbomb is structurally best at and every other tool is
worst at. It is also the reason the *distribution role* (R7) is not a nicety.

## R2 — The component's own licence text, verbatim

* MIT: "The above copyright notice and this permission notice shall be included
  in all copies or substantial portions of the Software."
* BSD-2/3-Clause: "Redistributions in binary form must reproduce the above
  copyright notice, this list of conditions and the following disclaimer in the
  documentation and/or other materials provided with the distribution."
* Apache-2.0 §4(a): "You must give any other recipients of the Work or
  Derivative Works a copy of this License."

The MIT and BSD texts name the rights holder **inside the text**. The generic
SPDX text for `MIT` carries a placeholder, so shipping it does not discharge
the obligation — it ships a template where a notice was required. An export
must therefore carry the bytes the component itself contains, and must never
substitute a canonical text for a missing one.

Consequence for sbomb: an SPDX identifier is not a deliverable. `MIT` is an
index into a catalogue; the attribution artifact needs the file.

## R3 — Copyright statements, verbatim

Required explicitly by MIT and BSD (above) and by Apache-2.0 §4(c) for source
form. The machine-readable form is REUSE / SPDX `SPDX-FileCopyrightText:`; the
conventional form is a `Copyright` line. A copyright notice must contain the
holder and should contain the year (REUSE 3.3).

Verbatim matters twice: a reformatted year range and a normalized holder are
both alterations of a notice that the licence says must be reproduced.

## R4 — NOTICE propagation, kept separate from licence text

Apache-2.0 §4(d): if the Work includes a NOTICE file, a derivative work must
include a readable copy of the attribution notices it contains — in a NOTICE
file, in the source or documentation, or in a display generated by the work.

The ASF adds two rules that shape the data model:

* NOTICE is for notices **not already satisfied** by the licence text. "The
  copyright notifications embedded within BSD and MIT licenses do not need to
  be duplicated in NOTICE."
* "Do not add anything to NOTICE which is not legally required."

So NOTICE content is a distinct kind of artifact with a distinct obligation,
and it must never be mistaken for licence-identification evidence. A NOTICE
file is poor evidence of *which* licence applies and strong evidence of *what
must be reproduced*.

## R5 — Modification status

* Apache-2.0 §4(b): "You must cause any modified files to carry prominent
  notices stating that You changed the files."
* GPL-2.0 §2(a), GPL-3.0 §5(a): the same in stronger form.
* MPL-2.0 §3.3, EPL-2.0 §3.2: notices on modified files.

An export must therefore report, per component, whether it was modified. Three
states, not two: `true`, `false`, and `unknown`. Reporting `unknown` as `false`
turns a missing check into a claim that no modification happened, which is the
one error an auditor will find.

## R6 — Source obligations are named, never produced

For the copyleft family the obligation is material sbomb does not have and
cannot derive:

| Licence | What is owed | Why usage evidence cannot produce it |
|---|---|---|
| GPL-2.0 §3, GPL-3.0 §6 | Complete Corresponding Source: the source of the whole work, plus the scripts used to control compilation and installation; GPL-3.0 adds Installation Information for consumer devices. A written offer must be valid for at least three years. | CCS is the complete source, not the subset the linker touched. Build scripts and interface definitions are not in the evidence graph. |
| LGPL-2.1 §6 | For static linking: the application's object files or source, plus what is needed to relink against a modified library, plus instructions. | sbomb records *that* an archive member was linked; it does not own the relinkable objects or the link line as a redistributable. |
| MPL-2.0 §3.2, EPL-2.0 §3.1, CDDL | Source of the covered files, on the same medium or via a stated mechanism. | Same reason. |

An evidence-derived subset of sources would *look* like a source offer while
being materially incomplete. That is a worse outcome than producing nothing:
it converts sbomb's precision into a compliance defect. The export therefore
names the obligation, its trigger and its consequence, and states plainly that
the material is out of scope — and the absence of such material is asserted by
a test, not by a promise (see [F7](milestones/F7-foss-command.md)).

## R7 — Distribution form is part of the trigger

Attribution obligations attach to a binary distribution for MIT, ISC, BSD and
Apache-2.0. Two mainstream licences are explicit exceptions: **Zlib** binds its
notice requirement to *source* distributions, and **BSL-1.0** permits the
notice to be omitted from machine-executable object code.

Two consequences:

* The role of the thing being described (a firmware image, an executable) must
  be recorded. sbomb already does this (`sbomb:artifact:role`).
* Stage 1 must **not** implement per-licence form exceptions. Deciding that a
  component may be omitted because zlib allows it is a legal conclusion. Name
  everything; let the manufacturer strike what they choose.

**Assets are distribution too.** A font under OFL-1.1 or an icon set under
CC-BY carries attribution obligations that do not care whether the file was
compiled. Embedded assets are therefore ordinary components in this export
(decision [Q18](decisions.md)). Two limits follow and must be stated rather
than discovered: CC-BY requires indicating whether changes were made, and sbomb
answers modification per component rather than per embedded blob; and OFL's
reserved font names are a naming rule sbomb cannot check at all.

## R8 — Machine-readable first, human-readable derived

Two independent reasons:

* **Regulatory.** BSI TR-03183-2 §5.2.5 requires the licence field to occur
  exactly once per component, as an SPDX identifier or expression. sbomb
  already targets this guideline; the FOSS data must not sit outside the
  document it certifies.
* **Interoperability.** CycloneDX 1.6 and 1.7 specify a field for each thing
  this export collects. Using them costs nothing and makes the data usable by
  every other tool in the chain:

| Datum | CycloneDX field | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Licence text, verbatim | `licenses[].license.text` / `evidence.licenses[].license.text` | `attachment`: `contentType`, `encoding: base64`, `content` |
| Declared vs. concluded | `license.acknowledgement` | `declared` \| `concluded`; maps directly onto §22.4 evidence classes |
| Copyright, observed | `evidence.copyright[].text` | array of `{text}` |
| Copyright, concluded | `component.copyright` | a single string; a conclusion, so curated only |
| Observed licences | `evidence.licenses` | already emitted by sbomb |
| Modification (R5) | `component.pedigree` | `commits`, `patches[]` with a type enum, `notes` — the specified place for "modified relative to upstream" |
| Build tool vs. shipped (R1) | `component.scope` | `excluded` means "not reachable at runtime"; close to the distribution role but not identical |

**Who reads this.** The attribution *document* has no standard and needs none —
the obligation is about content, and every vendor formats it differently. The
document does: Dependency-Track, `sbomqs`, TR-03183 conformance checkers, ORT
and ScanCode all consume CycloneDX or SPDX and none of them consume a notices
file. That is what makes the field-versus-property choice a compatibility
decision rather than a stylistic one.

SPDX has equivalents for most of it (`PackageLicenseDeclared` /
`PackageLicenseConcluded`, `PackageCopyrightText`, `PackageAttributionText`,
`ExtractedLicensingInfo`) and one significant gap: no clean slot for the
verbatim text of a *listed* licence as a component carries it, which is exactly
the MIT and BSD case. The detail is in
[spec-delta.md §8](spec-delta.md); it is why the binding is CycloneDX first and
why the SPDX writer of §36.1 will have a deviation to record.

sbomb's own §28.1 already settles the design question this raises: where the
format specifies a field, the field wins over a property. The licence text
belongs in `evidence.licenses[].license.text`, not in an `sbomb:` property and
not only in a text file beside the document.

## R9 — Self-contained delivery

A notices document must contain what it attributes. The ASF permits a pointer
only to material **inside the same distribution** ("For details, see
`deps/superwidget/`"). A hyperlink to a licence on the internet is not
delivery: the recipient's rights cannot depend on a third party's uptime.

So `license.url` may be recorded as a convenience and never as a substitute for
`license.text`.

## R10 — Incompleteness is stated, never papered over

Where the text or the copyright line could not be established, the export says
so at the point where the entry would have been. The alternatives are both
worse: a silent omission looks complete, and a substituted canonical text looks
correct. This is the same rule §22.7 already applies to NOASSERTION, extended
to the attribution artifact.

## R11 — Every entry is traceable to evidence

The manufacturer has to defend the document. Each retained artifact therefore
carries the canonical path it came from and its SHA-256, and each component's
entry is reachable from the same evidence chain as its SBOM entry. sbomb's
anchors, hashes and `explain` already provide this; the FOSS data must not
break out of it.

## R12 — Deterministic and diffable

Two runs of the same build produce the same bytes (§29), because a notices
document that churns cannot be reviewed, and a release-to-release diff is how a
new copyleft component gets noticed. Ordering is fixed, dedup keys are never
stored, and verbatim content is never rewritten — including line endings.

## What this does *not* require, and why it is out of scope

| Not required of the export | Why |
|---|---|
| A licence-compatibility verdict | A legal conclusion about a combination of terms. Non-goal §1.4. |
| An obligation-fulfilment record | sbomb cannot observe whether the notices file was actually shipped. |
| A source bundle or corresponding source | R6. Producing one would be a defect, not a feature. |
| Similarity or percentage licence matching | Forbidden by §22.3; a score is not evidence. |
| A licence database or any network lookup | §30.8. |
| Per-licence form exceptions (R7) | A legal conclusion; Stage 2 at the earliest, and only as customer-owned data. |
